# **4. Plant Procedures**

Nuclear power plant operators use written procedures to safely and consistently operate the plant. Procedures provide operators with step-by-step guidance for performing plant activities, responding to changing plant conditions, and taking appropriate actions when abnormal or emergency conditions occur.

Operating a nuclear reactor simulator such as RANCOR is no different. Procedures are an important part of the simulation because they help users understand what actions should be taken, when those actions should be taken, and what plant conditions should be monitored throughout an event.

During RANCOR simulations, users may encounter three primary types of procedures: **Operating Procedures (OPs), Abnormal Operating Procedures (AOPs), and Emergency Operating Procedures (EOPs).** Each type serves a different purpose depending on the condition of the simulated plant.

## **4.1 Operating Procedures (OP)**

**Operating Procedures (OPs)** provide instructions for normal plant operations. These procedures guide operators through routine activities and help ensure that tasks are performed in a consistent and controlled manner.

Operating Procedures may be used for activities such as:

- Plant or system startup
- Plant or system shutdown
- Normal system operation
- Changing equipment or system configurations
- Routine operator actions
- Placing equipment into or out of service
- Monitoring normal plant conditions

In the RANCOR simulation, OPs help users become familiar with normal plant operation before progressing to more complex abnormal or emergency scenarios.

## **4.2 Abnormal Operating Procedures (AOP)**

**Abnormal Operating Procedures (AOPs)** provide guidance when plant conditions have moved outside normal operation but have not necessarily developed into an emergency.

An abnormal condition may involve unexpected equipment behavior, changes in system parameters, instrument indications, alarms, or other conditions that require operator attention.

AOPs help operators identify the abnormal condition, determine the appropriate response, and take actions intended to return the plant to a normal or stable condition. Depending on the event, an AOP may also direct operators to another procedure if plant conditions continue to change.

Examples of situations that may require an AOP include:

- Unexpected equipment malfunctions
- Loss or degradation of a supporting system
- Abnormal temperature, pressure, level, or flow
- Unexpected alarms or instrument indications
- Problems with pumps, valves, or other plant equipment

## **4.3 Emergency Operating Procedures (EOP)**

**Emergency Operating Procedures (EOPs)** provide guidance for responding to serious plant conditions and accident scenarios. These procedures help operators take actions necessary to place the reactor and plant systems in a safe and stable condition.

During an emergency scenario, operators must evaluate plant indications, recognize changing conditions, and perform the actions directed by the applicable procedure. EOPs are designed to provide a structured response when normal or abnormal operating actions are no longer sufficient for the conditions being experienced.

Within RANCOR, EOP scenarios can help users practice interpreting plant conditions, responding to alarms, using instrumentation, manipulating controls, and understanding how operator actions affect the overall response of the simulated plant.

## **4.4 How the Procedures Connect**

A useful way to think about the three procedure categories is:

- **OP — Normal conditions:** Used to operate the plant and its systems during expected conditions.
- **AOP — Abnormal conditions:** Used when something is not operating as expected and operator action is required.
- **EOP — Emergency conditions:** Used during serious events when operators must respond to protect the reactor and maintain or restore safe plant conditions.

As you work through RANCOR training scenarios, pay attention not only to the actions listed in a procedure, but also to the **plant indications, alarms, and conditions that caused the procedure to be entered**. Understanding why a procedure is being used is an important part of understanding how the simulated plant operates.

## **4.5 Procedure Formats**

During RANCOR simulations, procedures may be available in two different formats: **Paper Procedures** and **Computer-Based Procedures (CBPs)**. Both formats are designed to provide the procedural guidance needed to operate the simulated plant and respond to different plant conditions.

Understanding how to navigate both formats is an important part of the training. Although the information and required actions may be similar, the way that information is presented and accessed can be different.

### **4.5.1 Paper Procedures**

**Paper Procedures** represent the traditional procedure format used in nuclear power plant operations. Many procedure sets were originally developed for paper use and may contain formatting, terminology, and organizational structures that differ from modern digital interfaces. This is an example of what a paper procedure looks like, for a reactor shutdown.

![Reactor Shutdown Paper Procedure](images/shutdown_P.png)

During RANCOR simulations, users may have access to these procedures as PDF documents. The procedures provide step-by-step instructions that guide the user through the actions required to accomplish a particular task or respond to a plant condition.

When using a paper procedure, users should become familiar with how information is organized and pay close attention to items such as:

- Procedure titles and numbers
- Prerequisites and initial conditions
- Individual procedure steps
- Cautions, warnings, and notes
- References to other procedures
- Conditional or branching instructions
- Required plant indications and parameters

Paper procedures may require users to move between different pages, sections, or referenced procedures as plant conditions change. Learning how to navigate this information efficiently is part of developing familiarity with procedure-based plant operation.

!!! tip "Using Paper Procedures"
    Take time to understand the structure of the procedure before beginning a simulation. Knowing where to find steps, cautions, references, and supporting information can make the procedure easier to follow during changing plant conditions.

### **4.5.2 Computer-Based Procedures**

**Computer-Based Procedures (CBPs)** provide procedural guidance through a digital interface rather than relying entirely on a traditional paper or PDF format. This is an example of what a computer-based procedures looks like. This procedure is for shutting down the reactor in an expedient way.

![Reactor Shutdown Computer-Based Procedure](images/shutdown_CB.png)

The goal of the computer-based format is to present procedural information in a **consistent, organized, and easy-to-navigate layout**. A digital format can also make it possible to present relevant information more efficiently, reducing the amount of material that must be viewed or navigated at one time.

Computer-Based Procedures may make it easier for users to:

- Identify the current procedure and step
- Navigate between procedure sections
- View important information in a consistent format
- Locate cautions, warnings, and notes
- Follow conditional or branching steps
- Access supporting information

The consistent format can be especially useful during training because users can spend more time focusing on **why an action is being performed and how the plant responds**, rather than learning a different document layout for each procedure.

!!! note "Development"
    Computer-Based Procedures for RANCOR are being developed and refined. Available features and procedure content may change as development continues.

## **4.6 Paper Procedures vs. Computer-Based Procedures**

Although the presentation is different, both formats serve the same fundamental purpose: **providing users with structured guidance for operating the simulated plant.**

| Paper Procedures | Computer-Based Procedures |
| --- | --- |
| Presented as traditional documents or PDFs | Presented through a digital interface |
| May contain different layouts depending on the procedure | Uses a more consistent presentation |
| Users manually navigate pages and sections | Digital navigation can simplify movement between information |
| Provides the complete procedural document | Can organize relevant procedural information more efficiently |
| Familiarizes users with traditional procedure use | Familiarizes users with a modern digital procedure environment |

As you work through RANCOR simulations, you may encounter either format. Becoming comfortable with both will help you focus on the procedure instructions, plant indications, and expected response rather than on the format in which the information is presented.
