# **5. Crew Roles and Responsibilities**

Safe and effective reactor operations depend on a well-coordinated operating crew. Each crew member has specific responsibilities, and clear communication between crew members helps maintain situational awareness, coordinate activities, and reduce the potential for errors.

During RANCOR training, users may encounter three primary crew roles: **Certified Observer (CO), Reactor Operator (RO), and Senior Reactor Operator (SRO)**. Each role represents a different level of responsibility within the operating crew.

## **5.1 Certified Observer (CO)**

The **Certified Observer (CO)** is an individual who is developing the knowledge and experience necessary to perform reactor operator duties.

The CO observes plant operations, follows the actions of qualified operators, and develops familiarity with areas such as:

* Plant systems and components
* Operating procedures
* Instrumentation and controls
* Alarms and plant indications
* Crew communication
* Operator responsibilities
* Normal, abnormal, and emergency plant conditions

The observer role provides an opportunity to learn how individual operator actions fit into the overall operation of the reactor and how members of the operating crew work together.

## **5.2 Reactor Operator (RO)**

The **Reactor Operator (RO)** is responsible for monitoring and operating assigned reactor systems and controls in accordance with applicable procedures and crew direction.

An RO must maintain awareness of plant conditions and communicate effectively with other members of the operating crew. This includes monitoring instruments and alarms, performing procedural actions, manipulating assigned controls, and reporting important changes in plant conditions.

### **5.2.1 Crew Communication**

Effective communication is an important part of reactor operations. One communication technique used during operations is **three-way communication**.

Three-way communication generally consists of three parts:

1. **Sender:** The first crew member clearly communicates the intended action or information.
2. **Receiver:** The second crew member repeats or acknowledges the information to confirm what was heard and understood.
3. **Sender Verification:** The original sender verifies that the information was understood correctly before the activity continues.

For example, an RO may communicate an intended action associated with a procedure step. The receiving crew member acknowledges or repeats the information, and the RO confirms that the communication was understood correctly before proceeding as appropriate.

This communication process helps reduce misunderstandings and ensures that crew members maintain a shared understanding of important plant activities.

### **5.2.2 Authority and Task Designation**

Before beginning an operating activity or simulation scenario, individual Reactor Operators may be assigned specific systems, equipment, controls, or responsibilities.

Clearly defining these responsibilities helps the crew understand **who is responsible for each activity** and prevents confusion when several tasks are being performed at the same time.

Task designation becomes particularly important during complex scenarios or situations involving multiple systems or reactors. Each operator should understand their assigned responsibilities while continuing to communicate significant plant conditions and actions to the rest of the operating crew.

## **5.3 Senior Reactor Operator (SRO)**

The **Senior Reactor Operator (SRO)** provides supervisory oversight and direction for reactor operations. The SRO maintains a broader view of plant conditions and coordinates the activities of the operating crew.

SRO responsibilities may include:

* Maintaining overall awareness of reactor and plant conditions
* Directing and coordinating crew activities
* Monitoring the progression of operating procedures
* Evaluating significant alarms and plant indications
* Assigning responsibilities to Reactor Operators
* Coordinating responses to changing plant conditions
* Ensuring important information is communicated among crew members
* Providing supervisory direction during normal, abnormal, and emergency conditions

While an RO may concentrate on assigned equipment, controls, or procedural actions, the SRO maintains a broader operational perspective and helps ensure that individual actions support the overall objectives of the operating crew.

## **5.4 Working as a Crew**

The CO, RO, and SRO roles work together to support effective plant operation. Successful crew performance depends not only on the technical knowledge of individual operators, but also on **communication, coordination, situational awareness, and clearly defined responsibilities**.

During RANCOR simulations, users should pay attention to both the technical actions being performed and the communication occurring between crew members. Understanding how information and responsibilities move through the operating crew is an important part of learning how coordinated reactor operations are performed.
